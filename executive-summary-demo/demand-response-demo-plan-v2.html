1The Opportunity

Background

Energy Company aggregates EV charging — next, heat and industrial assets — into a virtual power plant, selling flexibility into Nordic markets while guaranteeing every car is charged by departure. Thesis: capacity is the supply, margin is the monetization, trust is the license.

Current situation

Nordic capacity and volatility are both at record levels; transmission is delayed and battery capex keeps rising. Energy Company has 200,000+ EV customers in reach and a 4,218-vehicle pilot delivering 8.4 MW and €312K net savings YTD. Next 12–24 months decide Nordic flex market share.

Business challenges

  • Monetizing flexibility without tariff increases or rate cases
  • Making retail-scale assets bankable for the desk and TSOs
  • Protecting social license as dispatch events grow
  • Avoiding disconnected point solutions as the program expands
  • Proving delivered value against credible counterfactual baselines

3Business-Case Hypothesis

Today's pilot figures (4,218 EVs, 8.4 MW, €312K YTD, €42/customer, 94–96% delivery) are demonstrated; 100,000-EV scale is a hypothesis.

Baseline assumptions to confirm

  • ~2 kW average orchestrable flexibility per enrolled EV
  • Enrollment scales toward 100,000 EVs with opt-out at or below 1%
  • Blended value between €89K–€285K/MW/yr
  • One district-heating boiler adds 20–30 MW near-zero-CAC flexibility

Working value math

100,000 EVs × ~2 kW ≈ 200 MW, plus 20–30 MW from one boiler. At €89K–285K/MW/yr, roughly €18M–€57M/yr gross value.

Risk reduction

The closed loop runs today, so Phase-1 risk shrinks to data wiring and market onboarding.

Executive ask

Confirm the capacity–margin–trust scorecard, name first data sources to wire, and name IT/business co-sponsors this quarter.
