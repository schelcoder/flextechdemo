LOOP 1 An ordinary Tuesday

100,000 cars and one boiler become your next power plant.

So you can…

The Nordics have record generation capacity and record volatility. Transmission is delayed, battery capex keeps climbing, and the one lever that scales with renewables is the flexibility already sitting on your customers' side of the meter. I'll open with what that looks like as one orchestrated asset — so you can judge the size of the prize before we prove the machinery behind it.

SHOW The aggregated flexibility envelope

From one EV to a portfolio-scale asset.

  • 1One EV plugged in for the night — the small, familiar act of charging. Everything that follows is just this, multiplied and orchestrated.
  • 2Scroll: the fleet scales to 100,000 enrolled EVs and the flexibility band fans out to roughly 200 MW — on a usual weather pattern, no storm, no cold snap. This is an ordinary day.
  • 3A district-heating electric boiler backs down inside the same envelope — one industrial asset adding 20–30 MW of firm, dispatchable headroom. One boiler moves like thousands of cars, and it never drives away.
  • 4The revenue counter lands on the combined portfolio — stated as a hypothesis range — with every customer still charged by departure time.
SO WHAT

A capacity line that grows with renewables, not against them.

Every orchestrated megawatt is a megawatt of peaker capacity, grid reinforcement or curtailment you do not have to buy — a near-zero-marginal-cost capacity line. The rest of this session proves it is running machinery, not a concept video.

LOOP 2 The bankable number

Turn 100,000 customers into one number your desk can sell.

So you can…

A portfolio of small flexible loads is only bankable if the desk knows, at any moment, how many megawatts it can deliver now and an hour from now — and with what confidence. I'll show today's live desk view of the 4,218-vehicle fleet rolled up into exactly that number.

SHOW Portfolio forecast with confidence bands

Retail assets, market-grade confidence.

  • 1The trading dashboard: 8.4 MW available — 5.2 committed, 3.2 uncommitted — refreshed continuously, no overnight batch, no analyst middleware.
  • 2The fleet view: 4,218 enrolled, 3,247 connected right now, the state-of-charge distribution and the 17:00–19:00 plug-in wave.
  • 3The 48-hour flexibility forecast: confidence bands plotted against committed megawatts at 15-minute resolution — bid only what we're confident of, which is why delivery success runs 94–96%.
SO WHAT

Confidence bands make retail assets investable.

Confidence bands are what turn customers into capacity a transmission operator will pay for — you can declare firm megawatts with retail assets behind them. That credibility, not the megawatts alone, is what makes the 200,000-customer path investable.

LOOP 3 A new earnings line

No tariff increase. No rate case. No new plant.

So you can…

Flexibility revenue only survives a board meeting if every euro can be traced from market commitment through dispatch to settlement. I'll walk one dispatch event from bid to bank, audited at the same standard as generation revenue.

SHOW Dispatch-to-settlement traceability

Every euro, traced to the interval.

  • 1Market positions: the live bid book across five Nordic balancing and wholesale products, with accepted, pending and rejected bids at their clearing prices.
  • 2The dispatch-event log: target versus delivered megawatts, chargers dispatched, responses, opt-outs, revenue — variance sub-hourly, customer by customer, nothing hidden.
  • 3The program P&L: €312K net savings YTD at just €42 per customer per year — including a €42K annual lift found by rebalancing two reserve products.
SO WHAT

An EBITDA line the board — and the regulator — can trust.

This margin came from market participation, not from raising a single customer's tariff — and because it settles euro-by-euro against measured delivery, it's a number you can put in front of the board and the regulator with confidence.

LOOP 4 The social license

0.4% opt-out: the trust that lets the program scale.

So you can…

Every dispatch is a moment where a household either experiences value or feels coerced. I'll show exactly what your customer sees and the guarantees that protect them — so you can judge whether this earns the trust required to grow from 4,000 cars to 200,000.

SHOW Optimized charging, hard personal limits

Customers keep control. That's why they stay.

  • 1Tomorrow's price curve: the €350/MWh evening spike against roughly €11 overnight — the customer never has to understand this chart.
  • 2One night's optimized session: charging shifted overnight, €2.12 saved, and the car guaranteed at departure charge by 07:30.
  • 3The trust levers: minimum-charge guarantee, blackout windows, a one-tap override — alongside the truth: 4,218 enrolled, 0.4% opt-out, €312K returned to households.
SO WHAT

The scarce asset in this market is trust, not technology.

A 0.4% opt-out rate is proof the social license is holding. Whoever earns household trust first gets the 200,000-customer flexibility base; whoever loses it reads about themselves in the press.

LOOP 5 Run in minutes, defend for seven years

Growth without growing the governance risk.

So you can…

Scaling multiplies the ways this can go wrong — over-dispatching loyal customers, missing a vulnerable household, an unexplainable settlement. I'll show the program team's command view, where every risk arrives as a decision with its consequences modelled first.

SHOW A decision inbox with modelled what-ifs

Every action, a decision — not a dashboard.

  • 1The command bridge: live program KPIs and an action inbox — enrollment approvals, a fairness flag, a settlement dispute.
  • 2Open the pre-positioning decision for a hot Friday: available flexibility rising from 6.2 to 11.4 MW, the revenue lift, the modelled opt-out cost, and the exact customer message that would be sent.
  • 3The audit trail: every action recorded with actor, reason and hash-chained entries retained for seven years; vulnerable-customer protections at 100% compliance.
SO WHAT

Governance here is not a brake — it's what makes scale safe.

Fairness and auditability are what let you say yes to growth: the program moves at market speed while every decision stays explainable to a regulator, a journalist, or your own board.

LOOP 6 A pattern, not a project

One foundation carries heat, batteries and industrial flex.

So you can…

Everything you've seen — the consumer app, the trading desk, the command bridge — runs off one shared, governed data foundation. I'll show how each screen resolves to the same governed data, so phase two reuses this foundation instead of rebuilding it.

SHOW One governed foundation, end-to-end lineage

Three apps. One loop. One truth.

  • 1The flow in one picture: telematics, meters, prices, grid signals and weather land in one governed store; forecasts and baselines compute once; all three apps read the same conformed truth.
  • 2Trace one number end-to-end: the €312K on the P&L back through settlement records to individual dispatch intervals.
  • 3The expansion path: EV demand response now; heat and residential batteries next — the same boiler from the opening story; industrial and hydro-adjacent flex after.
SO WHAT

The next 12–24 months decide who owns Nordic flex.

Reusing one foundation is how you move at that speed. So our ask today: does the capacity–margin–trust thesis fit how you want the Energy Company data ecosystem to evolve — and who co-sponsors Phase 1?
